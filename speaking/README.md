# Speaking and technical briefings

Lloyd Clark, PhD, is Founder and CEO of [Federal.AI](https://federal.ai/). He brings more than 20 years in software and AI/ML, experience as a commercial pilot and certified flight instructor, and ownership of [Blue Ridge Electric Service](https://blueridge.ai/). His work focuses on making AI useful in operational decisions through reliable data, inspectable evidence, and clear human authority.

The following are proposed talk topics, available for discussion as executive briefings, technical sessions, or facilitated workshops. Examples use public material and synthetic scenarios.

## The economics of AI after the demo

Token cost tells only part of the story. This session works through a hypothetical business case that includes review, rework, rejected attempts, and ongoing operating cost. Participants examine how acceptance thresholds and reviewer effort change the result, then define a measurement plan for one real workflow.

**Audience:** Executives, program owners, operations leaders, and teams evaluating AI investments.

**Takeaways:** Define an accepted outcome; calculate cost per accepted item; distinguish released capacity from cash savings; identify the assumptions a pilot must test.

[Read the brief](../notes/06-cost-per-useful-outcome.md)

## Human approval as an engineering boundary

An agent proposes an action. A person approves it. What ensures that the same action executes once, before approval expires, against the expected state? This session follows a synthetic scheduling proposal through immutable records, approval binding, concurrency, idempotency, and uncertain external outcomes.

**Audience:** Engineering leaders, architects, AI builders, and governance teams designing systems that can act.

**Takeaways:** Specify approval authority; bind review to exact action content; design safe retries; test stale approvals and interrupted execution.

[Read the brief](../notes/07-approval-as-architecture.md)

## Proving AI can work without the internet

Disconnected operation requires more than local weights. This session outlines an evaluation that spans artifact inventory, clean installation, local evidence retrieval, repeatable scoring, network observations, and rollback. It separates task quality from deployment reliability and shows what an evaluation report can credibly claim.

**Audience:** Platform engineers, enterprise architects, and program teams working with constrained connectivity or local deployment requirements.

**Takeaways:** Define the deployment bundle; pin an evaluation environment; expose hidden network dependencies; report reproducibility and failure behavior accurately.

[Read the brief](../notes/08-disconnected-ai-evaluation.md)
